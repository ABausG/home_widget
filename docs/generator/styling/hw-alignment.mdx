# HWAlignment

Alignment enums consumed by [`HWColumn`](/generator/widgets/hw-column) and [`HWRow`](/generator/widgets/hw-row).

The cross-axis is the axis perpendicular to the layout direction:

- `HWColumn` (vertical) — cross-axis is **horizontal**, main-axis is **vertical**.
- `HWRow` (horizontal) — cross-axis is **vertical**, main-axis is **horizontal**.

## HWCrossAxisAlignment

| Value      | Description                          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `start`    | Align children to the cross-axis start. |
| `center`   | Center children along the cross-axis.   |
| `end`      | Align children to the cross-axis end.   |
| `baseline` | Line the children up on their first text baselines. **`HWRow` only.** |

Leaving `crossAxisAlignment` unset is the same as `center` on both platforms.

`baseline` is what a large display number next to a small caption usually wants: `end` aligns the boxes the two texts sit in, which leaves their baselines apart by the difference in descent. A column's cross axis is horizontal and has no baseline, so an `HWColumn` asking for `baseline` fails generation.

On Android it lines up the children that render text of their own, and only those. A child rendering none — a picture, an icon, a column of several texts — has no baseline to go by and stays at the top of the row, which is where a row with fewer than two texts leaves all of them; generation warns when that is what a row asks for. iOS has no such limit: SwiftUI's `.firstTextBaseline` puts the bottom edge of a child rendering no text on the baseline of the one that does.

A row holding a text in a [custom font](/generator/custom-fonts) is lined up by the plugin when the widget updates, since such a text is drawn into a bitmap that carries no baseline for the layout to read. The same goes for a row whose `spacing` puts a text with a background or a border in a `Box` of its own, which reports no baseline either.

```dart
HWRow(
  crossAxisAlignment: HWCrossAxisAlignment.baseline,
  children: [
    HWText(HWInt('streak'), style: HWTextStyle(fontSize: 34)),
    HWText.fixed('days', style: HWTextStyle(fontSize: 12)),
  ],
)
```

## HWMainAxisAlignment

| Value          | Description                                                       |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `start`        | Pack children at the main-axis start.                             |
| `center`       | Center children along the main-axis.                              |
| `end`          | Pack children at the main-axis end.                               |
| `spaceBetween` | Distribute children with equal space between them.                |
| `spaceEvenly`  | Distribute children with equal space between, before, and after.  |

Any value other than `start` stretches the `HWColumn` / `HWRow` along its main axis, since there has to be room to distribute. `start` and leaving it unset keep it hugging its children.

The alignment only distributes the room left over. The spacers it inserts shrink to nothing when there is none, so they never keep two children apart on their own; `spacing` is what guarantees a gap, and the alignment adds its share on top of it.

### Spacers and the Android child limit

Glance lays out at most 10 children in one `Column` or `Row`, and each spacer an alignment inserts counts as a child. For a stack of `n` children:

| Value           | Spacers | Children + spacers | Most children |
| --------------- | ------- | ------------------ | ------------- |
| `start` / unset | 0       | n                  | 10            |
| `end`           | 1       | n + 1              | 9             |
| `center`        | 2       | n + 2              | 8             |
| `spaceBetween`  | n − 1   | 2n − 1             | 5             |
| `spaceEvenly`   | n + 1   | 2n + 1             | 4             |

`spacing` adds nothing to the count. A stack Android would render with more than 10 fails generation; see [`HWColumn`](/generator/widgets/hw-column#android-at-most-10-children). For an `HWColumn.builder` or `HWRow.builder`, `n` is its `maxItems`, so the last column is the largest `maxItems` it can have; see [Lists](/generator/lists#android-limits).

## Platform mapping

- **iOS:** maps to SwiftUI [`HorizontalAlignment`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/horizontalalignment) / [`VerticalAlignment`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/verticalalignment) on the stack, with `Spacer(minLength: 0)`s inserted to model the `center`, `end`, `spaceBetween` and `spaceEvenly` values. Where those spacers might not stretch the stack — `spaceBetween` with a single child, or a builder, whose items are only known at runtime — a `.frame` with an infinite maximum stretches it and keeps the children at the start. `baseline` becomes `.firstTextBaseline`.
- **Android:** maps to Glance [`Alignment.Horizontal`](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/glance/layout/Alignment.Horizontal) / [`Alignment.Vertical`](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/glance/layout/Alignment.Vertical) on the `Column` / `Row`, with weighted `Spacer`s for the `center`, `end`, `spaceBetween` and `spaceEvenly` values.
